S1: Calculating quality factor of boron nitride nanostructures
Along with the size of BNNS, it is also important to determine its quality. We consider 
that any discontinuity in the continuous form of ring structures, non-6-membered rings and 
defects degrade the quality of the BNNS. All these factors typically result in reducing the 
number of 6-membered rings in the structure. Hence, to determine the quality factor, we 
compare the optimal number of 6-membered rings possible with the actual number of 6-
membered rings in the structure. 
Figure A.1 briefly explains the calculation of optimal number of 6-membered rings for a given 
set of molecules. As color coded in the figure, the 1st hexagon needs 6 particles or in our case 
3 B and 3 N atoms to form. The formation of 2nd hexagon in any direction attached to the 1st 
hexagon will need additional 4 particles. The 3rd hexagon can be added to this structure by 
using either 3 or 4 more particles. However, new hexagons can be added to the structure of first 
two rings infinitely by using 3 more particles for each additional hexagon.
