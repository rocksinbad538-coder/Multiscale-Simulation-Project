# Purpose

This document records the major scientific and technical decisions taken during the development of the project.

Unlike the daily logs, this document intentionally omits routine work and focuses only on milestones that changed the scientific direction of the project.

Its purpose is to preserve the reasoning behind every important decision so that the project can be resumed at any point without losing continuity.

---

# Milestone 1
## Definition of the scientific architecture

The first stage of the project consisted of reviewing the original proposal and defining the computational architecture required to achieve the objectives described in the Master Project Document.

Several possible strategies were evaluated.

Among them:

- phenomenological models;
- simplified coarse-grained descriptions;
- fully atomistic simulations.

Decision:

Adopt a fully atomistic multiscale workflow.

Reason:

The final deliverables require physically meaningful optical and excitonic properties that cannot be obtained from simplified phenomenological models alone.

---

# Milestone 2
## Selection of the structural scaffold

Several scaffold candidates were evaluated.

Final decision:

Use an atomistic h-BN nanotube as the artificial microtubule-inspired scaffold.

Reasons:

- chemically realistic
- mechanically stable
- compatible with MD
- compatible with DFT
- compatible with TDDFT
- compatible with future electromagnetic calculations

This became the structural basis of the entire project.

---

# Milestone 3
## Functionalization strategy

Chromophores were incorporated into the nanotube.

Explicit solvent was retained.

Explicit confined water was retained.

The system was intentionally designed to preserve atomistic realism rather than replacing the environment by dielectric continuum models.

---

# Milestone 4
## Molecular dynamics workflow

The MD protocol became the source of all subsequent electronic calculations.

Instead of computing excited states from a single optimized structure, the workflow was redesigned to use an ensemble of MD configurations.

Scientific consequence:

Electronic properties become time dependent.

This enables construction of a dynamic excitonic Hamiltonian.

---

# Milestone 5
## Local QM cluster philosophy

Rather than performing TDDFT on the complete nanotube, local QM clusters were introduced.

Each cluster contains

- one chromophore;
- nearby water molecules;
- surrounding electrostatic environment represented by point charges.

Advantages:

- computationally feasible;
- chemically realistic;
- scalable to production.

---

# Milestone 6
## Electrostatic embedding

A complete embedding workflow was developed.

Automatically generated:

- QM geometries
- point-charge files
- ORCA inputs
- manifests
- execution directories

Initially several formatting problems were identified.

Among them:

- point-charge ordering
- file formatting
- ORCA parsing
- LEANSCF failures

Each issue was individually diagnosed and corrected.

The final workflow became fully automatic.

---

# Milestone 7
## Embedded TDDFT validation

A pilot study was executed.

Frames selected:

- frame000
- frame010
- frame020

Chromophores:

- PYR2
- PYR3
- PYR4
- PYR5

Initial failures occurred due to point-charge formatting.

The embedding files were corrected.

After correction:

100% successful calculations.

This represented the first validation of the complete MD → TDDFT pipeline.

# Milestone 8
## Production workflow

Following pilot validation, the workflow was promoted to production.

Additional frames:

001

002

003

were processed automatically.

Outcome:

All calculations terminated normally.

No manual intervention was required.

This demonstrated that the computational infrastructure is robust enough for production-scale calculations.

# Milestone 12
## Scientific observations

Several consistent trends emerged.

PYR5 remains systematically red-shifted relative to the other chromophores.

Frame-to-frame fluctuations remain moderate.

No abnormal energy jumps have been observed.

Hamiltonian traces remain stable across sampled frames.

These observations support the physical consistency of the workflow.
